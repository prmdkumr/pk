Research

My PhD at IIT Roorkee, supervised by Prof. Ramesh Chandra, addresses one question: how can MXenes be made without hydrofluoric acid and still perform in real devices? The work links synthesis chemistry, thin-film growth and electrochemistry.

Background: 2H-MoS2, top view

Sustainable synthesis of MXenes

Conventional MXene synthesis relies on HF etching, which limits scale-up and safe use. I work on fluoride-free alternatives and on growing MXene-derived films directly by physical vapour deposition.

  • A sustainable synthesis route for Mo2C MXene that reduces gallium wastage, applied to energy storage devices (Chemical Engineering Journal, 2026).
  • Development of HF-free MXene during a Commonwealth Split-Site Fellowship at the Advanced Technology Institute, University of Surrey (2024–2025).
  • Pulsed-laser-deposited Ti-based MXene thin films, protected by Indian Patent No. 600290 (granted 2026).

Thin-film electrodes for energy storage

Sputtering and related PVD methods give binder-free electrodes with controlled crystallinity and thickness, which suits miniaturized and on-chip storage.

  • Crystalline MoO2 films as high-capacity, stable supercapacitor electrodes, with a mechanistic study of charge storage (Electrochimica Acta, 2024).
  • Sputter-grown TiCrN microelectrodes for a high-voltage (>3 V) miniaturized storage device.
  • Binder-free LiCoO2 and V2O5 thin-film cathodes and a Li1+xAlxTi2−x(PO4)3 solid electrolyte for thin-film lithium-ion micro-batteries.

Probing MXene electrodes in operation

To understand why partially oxidized MXenes store charge the way they do, I use synchrotron spectroscopy alongside lab XPS and electrochemical analysis.

Diamond Light Source beamtime (SI42094-1), Co-PI

Unraveling the ion accumulation and electronic states in partially oxidized Mo2C MXene electrodes during electrochemical processes.

Printed electronics and sensors

Before the PhD I worked on graphene inks and device fabrication, which shapes how I think about processing MXenes for devices.

  • M.Tech thesis on graphene conductive ink for printable electronics, including the effect of triple roll milling on ink rheology and conductivity.
  • Electrochemical sensors for point-of-care diagnostics at CeNSE, IISc Bangalore, and on-the-spot soil sensors at the Design Innovation Centre, IIT Roorkee.
  • A 2.4 and 5.8 GHz dual-band rectenna for RF energy harvesting, and graphene antennas, under the Akshay Urja project.
