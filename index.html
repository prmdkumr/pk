Pramod Kumar

PhD, Institute Instrumentation Centre, IIT Roorkee. Commonwealth Split-Site Fellow at the Advanced Technology Institute, University of Surrey.

I develop sustainable, HF-free routes to 2D transition metal carbides (MXenes) and grow them as thin films for energy storage, sensors and electronic devices.

Research focus

Sustainable MXene synthesis

Fluoride-free etching routes for Mo2C and Ti-based MXenes, including a process that cuts gallium wastage and pulsed-laser-deposited MXene thin films (granted patent).

Thin-film energy storage

Sputter-grown electrodes such as MoO2 and TiCrN for supercapacitors and micro-devices, and binder-free thin films for solid-state lithium-ion micro-batteries.

Sensors and printed electronics

Graphene conductive inks, electrochemical point-of-care sensors, MXene–graphene biosensors and RF energy harvesting from my M.Tech and early research years.

From graphene to MXenes

My work has moved through three 2D materials, each building on the processing lessons of the one before.

Graphene

2016 to 2020

Conductive inks for printable electronics, laser-patterned paper resistors, flexible FETs and strain sensors, and graphene antennas for RF energy harvesting.

Ti3C2Tx MXene

2021 onwards

MXene–graphene nanohybrid films for electrochemical biosensing, and Ti-based MXene thin films grown by pulsed laser deposition (granted patent).

Mo2C MXene

2021 onwards

HF-free synthesis that reduces gallium wastage, electrodes for energy storage, and synchrotron study of partially oxidized Mo2C at Diamond Light Source.

At a glance

Twenty peer-reviewed journal articles, including work in Chemical Engineering Journal, Nano Letters and Journal of Power Sources. Two granted Indian patents on thin-film coatings and PLD-grown MXene films. Co-PI on a Diamond Light Source beamtime studying ion accumulation in Mo2C MXene electrodes.
